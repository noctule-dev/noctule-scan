# Lessons

Things learned while building Noctule Scan, so neither of us relearns them the hard way. Add an entry whenever something surprises us, costs time, or changes how we work. Newest first within each section. Each entry gets a date, what happened, and what to do about it.

## Scanner hardware and protocol

- **2026-11-18: The spec's quick-key reply shape is wrong, and `GSI` gives the scanning list a different quick key (quick-keys spike, all three models).** Uniden's spec says `SQK` and `DQK` replies repeat the list and system keys before the 100 states; on the SDS150, SDS100 and SDS200 they carry the 100 states only. `GSI`'s `MonitorList` reported `Q_Key` 0 or 1 for a list whose `GLT` key is 9, the same list by `Index`. Two systems' departments have `GLT` keys that `DQK` reports as nonexistent, identically on all three. Separately, the SDS100 didn't answer `MDL` twice (port present), then answered everything on a later try, cause unseen.
  - What to do: parse replies from what the hardware sent, not from the spec's examples; match the scanning list, system and department by `Index`, never by quick key; give the mock the hardware's shapes, including the odd ones, so a client that relies on the spec fails there first. A handheld that doesn't answer may be busy on its own screen: try once more later, never in a loop.

- **2026-11-16: The SDS100 answers `KAL` with `ERR`, and a tool that hides replies couldn't show it (SDS100 check, fw 1.26.01).** The SDS100 stops sending `PSI` pushes about 182–186 s after the subscription starts, as the SDS150 does at 183 s. A first try of the SDS150's remedy used `noctule watch` with `KAL` every 60 s: the pushes stopped at 185 s, which looked like "`KAL` doesn't work" but proved nothing about what was written or what came back, because `watch` shows only pushes. The owner asked for the method to be confirmed. The SDS150's own keep-alive check, pointed at the SDS100, settled it: three `KAL`s written, each answered `ERR` (an unknown command), last push at 181.6 s. The same check with the subscription's `PSI,1000` sent again every 60 s passed (pushes to the end of a 240 s hold, each `PSI` answered `PSI,OK`). Uniden's spec lists `KAL` for the whole SDS series; the firmware decides. `GCS` (in the spec since 2024) gave no battery reading either: one `GCS` through `noctule info` ended in `no answer to GCS`, which again can't tell an `ERR` from silence.
  - What to do: a command in the spec is not a command in every model's firmware; try it on each model before a profile relies on it. To test whether a command has an effect, use a check that counts what it wrote and reports every line that came back, not one that shows only the happy path's output. When a new model is checked against another's result, run the other's proof on it, not a lookalike.

- **2026-11-16: The SDS100 and the SDS150 share a USB product ID, and the SDS100 has no USB serial number.** Both are vendor 0x1965, product 0x001A; only the product name (`SDS100 Serial Port`, `SDS150 Serial Port`) and the device release tell them apart, so a lookup by product ID labels an SDS100 as an SDS150. The SDS100 reports no serial number, so macOS names its port from the USB location and the path changes with the socket; the SDS150's placeholder serial keeps its path fixed. With both plugged in, everything that takes "the only Uniden port" (a station with no saved port, a bare `--serial`, the SDS150 hardware suites) finds two and stops, and the rule "the only Uniden port on this Mac is the SDS150" no longer holds.
  - What to do: identify a handheld by its USB product name, then confirm with `MDL` before anything else is sent. In a hardware run name the port explicitly and check it against the registry by product name just before each run (`ioreg` reads only; nothing opened). Never assume a saved port for the SDS100 survives a change of socket. Two radios of the same model can't be told apart at all: neither USB nor Uniden's spec gives a serial number.

- **2026-11-12: A recovery that only works because of an assumption in the mock (second whole-branch review of Plan 7).** The keep-alive passed its tests, its QA and an hour on hardware, and the review still found a hole none of them could show: if the Mac sleeps under `noctule log` for more than 183 s, the SDS150 stops pushing, and what brings the pushes back on wake is the next `KAL`. That a `KAL` starts stopped pushes again is something the mock simply does; hardware has only shown that a new `PSI` does. The reviewer proved it by changing the mock's answer in a throwaway worktree: with the pessimistic rule `log` kept running, kept sending `KAL`, and logged nothing. The same review found that the mock's SDS150 never sends the document the real one sends most (a named conventional channel with its frequency and no signal), so nothing on the mock can show what the app's headline does with it.
  - What to do: list every assumption the mock makes about a new behaviour, and for each ask which product path works only if it holds; give the mock a switch for the other answer and test under both, or put the question on the hardware list (design §8 check 16, §13 P). When hardware has shown one way to recover (a new `PSI`), build the recovery on that one, not on the untried one. Make the mock's idle document a model's captured idle document, not another model's.

- **2026-11-12: The SDS150 stops sending `PSI` pushes 183 s after the subscription starts, with the port still open (hardware session, fw 1.01.02).** `noctule log` on the owner's SDS150, left alone, printed `warning: scanner stopped sending updates` and never `scanner disconnected`. Timed three times with `noctule watch --serial` (which exits 5 s after the last update): pushes came with no gap of 2 s and then stopped for good, 183 s after the first, at `PSI,500` (356 pushes, twice) and at `PSI,1000` (181 pushes). So it is a time limit inside the scanner, not a push count and not our reader. The scanner answered commands afterwards, the port was quiet, and a new `PSI` started pushes again at once. Uniden's V2.00 spec states no timeout anywhere; it lists `KAL` ("Keep Alive", no reply) and nothing about what it keeps alive. Not yet known: whether the 183 s count from the last command of any kind or from the `PSI`, and what resets them (`KAL`, any command, or only a new `PSI`). The earlier checks all passed because none ran three minutes: `watch --count 20` takes 10 s, the push suite 7 s, and every mock test less. The design had the right check (an hour of `watch`, §8 check 6), listed sixth.
  - What fixed it (later the same day): the owner approved `KAL` over USB, the client now sends it every 60 s while subscribed (design §13 O), and on hardware the pushes went on past 183 s: `SDS150HardwareTests/KeepAlive` held 240 s (239 pushes, four `KAL`s, nothing sent back), a `watch --serial` ran 300 s, and `noctule log` ran its full hour with no stall (21 transmissions logged). Only `KAL`, at 60 s, was tried.
  - What to do: when a new transport or model first carries a subscription, run the long soak before the short checks are called a pass, and when a link goes quiet, time it twice before theorising: the same time to the second, twice, says the device does it on purpose. Once the rule is known, give the mock's SDS150 personality the same limit, so a station that only works for three minutes fails a test. The mock's SDS150 now stops pushing 183 s after the `PSI` or the latest `KAL`. (History: until the keep-alive was built, `watch` and `log` on a USB station were good for three minutes, and `KAL` waited for the owner's go-ahead, given on 2026-11-12.)

- **2026-11-12: The SDS150 logged no conventional hits, because `ScannerInfo` read a frequency only from `SiteFrequency` (hardware session, fw 1.01.02).** `noctule log` on the owner's SDS150 logged nothing while it stopped on a conventional transmission, and `noctule watch` showed `- MHz` on every named conventional channel. The SDS150 puts a conventional channel's `Freq`, `Mod` and `SAD` on `ConvFrequency` itself, with no `Site` or `SiteFrequency`, so `frequencyMHz` was nil, `hasActiveChannel` false, and `HitDetector` never opened a hit. The repository already held that shape: `sds150-gsi-serial.txt`, captured and sanitized the day before, though the design's §3.2 said only "Same structure as the SDS200". Nothing caught it because the conventional tests all used the SDS200's shape (`ConvFrequency` with a name and index plus a separate `SiteFrequency`), the mock's SDS150 personality copied the SDS200's documents apart from line breaks and the footer, and every serial test ran the trunk scenario. Now each attribute falls back to `ConvFrequency` when the SDS200's element doesn't carry it, the SDS150 personality writes the SDS150's conventional shape (`MockPersonality.conventionalForm`), and `StationRunnerUSBTests.usbStationLogsConventionalHits` fails without the fix. Still unknown: what `Property` (`Sig`, `Mute`, `Rssi`) says during a conventional transmission on the SDS150; `noctule status` now prints `signal:` and `muted:` to find out, and `info` prints an unknown battery state's `CST` code (the owner's unit answered one outside 0–6). Answered later that day with the fix in: a 170 s `noctule log` logged 8 real conventional transmissions, each started and ended, so `Sig` does rise above 0 and fall back; and the battery code is `CST=8` at 100 % on USB power.
  - What to do: when a capture from a new model arrives, diff its shape element by element and attribute by attribute against what the parser reads and against the mock personality, and make the personality reproduce every difference the capture shows, as data. Give each scan mode (trunk, conventional, weather) a test over each transport, at the level the user sees (a hit logged), not only a parser test on the SDS200's shape.

- **2026-11-11: The SDS150 doesn't exist on USB until Serial Port is chosen on its screen (fw 1.01.02; SDS150 design §3.1).** Plugged in, the scanner asks on its own screen whether to be a serial port or mass storage, and until that is answered the Mac sees no USB device at all: nothing in IOKit, no `/dev/cu.*` node. In serial mode it is "SDS150 Serial Port", vendor ID 0x1965, product ID 0x001A, two CDC interfaces and nothing else (no audio, no network), driven by macOS's own CDC ACM driver as `/dev/cu.usbmodem<serial>1`. Its USB serial number looks like a placeholder, so it can't tell two units apart. Mass storage is a different USB personality, not examined. So "no port" usually means the prompt is unanswered, the cable is out, the battery is flat, or the user chose mass storage, not a broken scanner; and a handheld does all of those routinely, which is why a USB station waits for its scanner and resumes (design §4.5).
  - What to do: when no port is found, say `Plug the scanner in and choose Serial Port on its screen.` (`SerialPort.noneFoundHint`); list ports with `noctule ports` (registry reads only, nothing opened) before guessing a path; never use the serial number to pick a unit. Keep real port paths and serial numbers out of tracked files.
- **2026-11-11: The SDS150 answers `MDL,SDS150`, not the `SDS150GBT` Uniden's spec and the `sds200` library give (fw 1.01.02).** Read-only probes of the owner's unit got `MDL,SDS150` and `VER,Version 1.01.02`. The V2.00 command specification, and the protocol notes before today (from [lib]), say `SDS150GBT`; a check for the spec's string alone would have called the owner's own scanner unknown.
  - What to do: compare models through `ScannerModel(reported:)`, which takes both names, never by the `MDL` string; the mock answers `SDS150` and `--mdl-name SDS150GBT` gives the spec's form, and both must pass (`SDS150HardwareTests` checks both on the mock). Treat any reply format quoted from the spec, not seen on hardware, as a guess.
- **2026-11-11: Over serial a `GSI` document has no `<Footer>`, so a reply ends where its root element closes (fw 1.01.02).** The SDS150 answered `GSI,<XML>,\r`, then the XML as lines separated by `\r`, ending `</ScannerInfo>\r`, with no `<Footer No=… EOT=…>`. Over UDP the end of a datagram ends a reply; on a serial byte stream nothing does, and the protocol notes' footer (`EOT="1"`, from [lib]) can't be relied on: the owner's SDS200 put none on `GSI` over UDP either (2026-11-08), only on `MSI`. The design's §3.2 says "UDP has one"; for `GSI` that was never seen. Also per the V2.00 spec (not yet seen on hardware), `GCS` ends with `\n`, not `\r`.
  - What to do: find serial reply boundaries by structure (`SerialResponseAssembler`: lines cut at `\r` or `\n`, a `NAME,<XML>,` line opens a document that ends when its root closes, a `<Footer>` if one ever comes is ignored), never by a footer, a terminator alone, or a pause; test the assembler with the bytes split at every position. Whether a push can land inside another reply over serial is unknown (design §8 check 3; `SDS150HardwareTests`' push suite looks for it).

- **2026-11-10: `contains("\r")` misses `"\r\n"`, so `ScannerCommand` accepted a CRLF inside an argument (Plan 6 final review I3).** In Swift `"\r\n"` is one `Character` (a grapheme cluster), equal to neither `"\r"` nor `"\n"`, so `argument.contains("\r") || argument.contains("\n")` let `"a\r\nb"` through: `ScannerCommand("MSV", "", "a\r\nb")` put `MSV,,a\r\nb\r` on the wire, a CR in the middle of a command. `MenuInput` already checked by scalar (the 2026-11-09 CSV entry is the same trap), so the app couldn't reach it, but the protocol layer's own guarantee was false. Also found there: `MenuModel` caught the client's `invalidArgument` but the refusal is `ScannerCommandError`, thrown while the command is built, so a refused value would have surfaced as "no answer" after a display nudge and a re-read. What to do: test for control characters by Unicode scalar (`unicodeScalars.contains`), never by `Character`, and test a refusal path with the error type the lower layer really throws.
- **2026-11-10: A "reply + delay" debounce with no cap starves under key repeat (Plan 6 final review I2).** In a menu the display re-read `STS` 150 ms after each key reply. A held scroll key's replies came about every 80 ms, so each reply pushed the read past the next reply: 38 presses in 3.2 s gave 38 `KEY` and no `STS`, and the screen only moved after release. Now the deferral is capped (`DisplayRefreshPolicy.menuMaxKeyDeferral`, 400 ms after the last read). What to do: any "wait until things settle" timer that each new event resets needs a maximum wait; test it with events arriving faster than the delay, not one at a time.
- **2026-11-10: Menus are remote-controllable through `MSI` and `MSV` (fw 1.26.01; design `docs/specs/2026-11-10-remote-menus-design.md` §4).** `MSI` describes the whole current menu screen (every item, not only the nine on the display) and `MSV,,<value>` acts on the current screen: an item index on a list, the whole text on an entry. An action can leave the menu (saving a number entry resumed scanning), so only a re-read says where you are. About 1 UDP command in 12 went unanswered, and the unanswered `KEY` had not taken effect, so a lost request and a lost reply look the same. `MSI` omits items it can't remote-control (Analyze, Customize, Auto Locate) and answers `TypeError` on screens it doesn't cover.
  - What to do: never resend a state-changing command (`KEY`, `MSV`) after a timeout, and never turn one user action into several keys. Never send an `MSV` value with a comma, CR or LF. Treat the root `Value` on lists as stale after an `MSV`; use `MenuItem/@Value` and `Selected`. `MNU` and `MSB` stay untried.

- **2026-11-09: The owner set the reconnect cooldown after a clean close per station, 0-120 s (Plan 5).** The fixed 120 s wait got in their way. Their reasoning: a controlled close (TEARDOWN answered, connection closed gracefully) is safe to follow at once; the hangs seen so far came from crashes, dropped connections and leaked sessions. **This is unverified on hardware**; the owner tests values themselves. So only a *clean* close (a session set up on that connection, every TEARDOWN answered 2xx, every request answered, none pending at `close()`, closed by `close()`) uses the station's `reconnectCooldownSeconds`; every other close waits the full 120 s, and a clean close never hides an unclean one. A connection that never had a session counts as unclean because a quick reconnect after such a "health-check" connection is one of the hangs we saw (2026-11-08). The mock's `--cooldown-after unclean` models the theory for tests; it is not evidence.
  - What to do: agents never lower a station's wait (or pass `--reconnect-cooldown` below 120) against the real scanner unless the owner asks. The test overrides (`--cooldown-seconds`, `NOCTULE_COOLDOWN_SECONDS`) set only the full value and stay mock-only. If a hang follows a clean close on hardware, record it here: it would disprove the theory.

- **2026-11-08: `noctule listen` ran four sessions on the SDS200 back to back, with the file-backed cooldown spacing them (fw 1.26.01).** Sessions: 10 s with playback, 10 s recorded to WAV, Ctrl-C about 5 s in, then 3 s. Runs 2-4 started at once and the CLI waited 113 s, 110 s, and 112 s on its own (`~/Library/Caches/NoctuleScan/rtsp-cooldown.json`). Every session had 0 lost packets (265, 255, 127, 77). The Ctrl-C run exited 130 about 1 s after the signal. `afinfo` read the WAV as 1 ch, 8000 Hz, Int16, 10.2 s. `MDL` answered before the first run and 10 s after the last.
  - What to do: rely on the cooldown store rather than hand-timing gaps between runs, and never delete the file to skip a wait. The CLI doesn't show TEARDOWN's status code (`AudioSession` doesn't expose it); the evidence of a clean close is the exit code, no "could not be closed" line, and the next session working.

- **2026-11-08: `MSM,1` restarts the SDS200; it came back about 10.5-12.7 s later (fw 1.26.01).** The command is undocumented (from the ProScan developer on RadioReference; the owner approved sending it). It replied `MSM,OK`, the UDP control channel then went silent, and `MDL` answered again about 10.5 s after the send in the controller's probe. `RestartHardwareTests` saw it back 12.7 s after the send (2 s polling), and a read-only `GSI` worked.
  - What to do:
    - Restart only after explicit user confirmation, never automatically. Use `ScannerRestart.perform`: it sends `MSM,1`, waits for `MDL` (requiring one silent poll first, or 15 s, because the scanner can answer briefly after `MSM,OK`), then clears the RTSP cooldown record.
    - Treat `MSM,OK` or no reply as success (the scanner may reboot first); `NG`/`ERR` is a failure.
    - It works only while UDP control answers. A fully hung network stack needs a power cycle, which `AudioSession.restartHint` (`.canRestart` vs `.powerCycleNeeded`, from an injected `MDL` probe) tells the UI.
    - Untested: whether a restart revives an RTSP server that was wedged while control still answered, and the first RTSP connect after a restart.

- **2026-11-08: Even with a 120 s or longer reconnect gap, the Network.framework audio path hung the SDS200; BSD sockets that mirror the proven Python client don't (fw 1.26.01).** This is separate from the quick-reconnect hazard (next entry), which hangs the scanner whatever the implementation: the controller's Python client triggered it too.
  - Evidence:
    - The controller's isolation runs: a Python client on plain BSD sockets ran the same RTSP/RTP exchange cleanly, including two 5 s sessions 2 minutes apart, a 60 s session with keepalives, and a 60 s session with keepalives plus `GSI` every 1 s. Control kept answering.
    - Swift runs sending byte-identical requests through `NWConnection` (RTSP) and `NWListener` (RTP) left the scanner's whole network service hung: 554 timed out, UDP control stopped answering, ping still worked, and it needed a power cycle.
    - After `RTSPClient` moved to `TCPStream` and `RTPReceiver` to `UDPReceiveSocket` (Task 5b), one `AudioHardwareTests` run passed both sessions: 60 s with `GSI` polling and keepalives, the 121 s cooldown, then a ~3 s session. Both TEARDOWNs got 200. `MDL` answered before the run and 10 s after it.
  - What to do:
    - Keep the audio path (RTSP TCP and RTP UDP) on BSD sockets. Don't reintroduce `NWConnection` or `NWListener` there without a hardware A/B test.
    - Close the way the reference client does: wait 0.5 s (after the TEARDOWN reply, and on every other graceful close of an open connection too, e.g. after a failed DESCRIBE; final review 2026-11-08), `shutdown(SHUT_WR)`, drain until EOF (at most 1 s), then `close()`. Never RST (`SO_LINGER` 0), and never close with unread data, which also sends RST. Close the RTP socket after that, 0.5 s later.
    - Which Network.framework behavior the scanner objects to is not isolated. Candidates are how `cancel()` ends the TCP connection, Network.framework's TCP option defaults, and the per-remote connections `NWListener` makes for UDP. This is one passing run, not a soak.
    - `UDPTransport` (control) is still on `NWConnection`, and it polled `GSI` through the passing run without trouble.

- **2026-11-08: Opening a new TCP connection to the SDS200's port 554 soon after the last one closed hangs its audio server until a power cycle (fw 1.26.01).**
  - Evidence:
    - An immediate second session failed to connect.
    - A health-check connection followed about 1 s later by a session hung the server. Both came from the controller's Python BSD-socket client, so this hazard doesn't depend on the networking implementation.
    - Session A (5 s), a 120 s wait, then session B: both worked.
    - A 60 s session (keepalives every 15 s, GSI polling), then a 121 s wait, then a connect: that failed too. So 120 s is a floor, not a proven bound. That run used Network.framework; the same sequence passed on BSD sockets (see the entry above), so the close itself may have been the problem.
  - What to do:
    - `RTSPClient` enforces `reconnectCooldown` (default 120 s) through an `RTSPCooldownStore`. The in-memory store is shared per process; `FileRTSPCooldownStore` at `~/Library/Caches/NoctuleScan/rtsp-cooldown.json` covers the CLI, the app, and tests.
    - Every close is recorded, including failed connects and dead connections. A teardown reconnect inside the cooldown leaves the ids in `unreleasedSessions` rather than connecting.
    - Never open a side connection to 554 to health-check.
    - Keep one session alive, and treat any reconnect as risky.
    - After a hang, stop and ask the owner for a power cycle.
- **2026-11-08: SDS200 RTSP needs the exact working request shape; an unanswered request can kill the audio server until a power cycle.**
  - On firmware 1.24.00, our first request shape (no OPTIONS, `User-Agent` last) got no reply to DESCRIBE, twice. Each time port 554 then refused connections until the owner power-cycled the scanner. The root cause was not isolated: the firmware, the missing OPTIONS, and the header order are all candidates. A host-name URL is not required to trigger it.
  - On firmware 1.26.01 the reference shape works: OPTIONS first, then `CSeq`, then `User-Agent`, then the method's own headers, with an IP-literal URL. `RTSPClient` now sends exactly that (`handshakeBytesMatchTheWorkingReferenceExchange` pins the bytes), and `options()` must be called before `describe()` on every connection.
  - DESCRIBE, SETUP, and PLAY each take about 0.5 s.
  - Never reconnect quickly (see the cooldown entry above).
  - Never retry in a loop. After any hang, stop and tell the owner.
- **2026-11-08: SDS200 1.24.00 over UDP: `PSI` is acknowledged, and `GSI` fits in one datagram.** A one-off read-only probe found that `PSI,1000` gets a `PSI,OK\r` line within about 15 ms, then `PSI,<XML>,` pushes about once a second. `PSI,0` also gets `PSI,OK`, and the pushes stop. A `GSI` reply in trunk scan mode is a single datagram of about 1 KB (`GSI,<XML>,\r<?xml ...`) with no `<Footer>`, so fragment reassembly wasn't exercised; it may still happen with larger documents. `ScannerClient.startPush` doesn't wait for the `PSI,OK`, and the router drops it harmlessly. The opt-in `HardwareTests` passed against this scanner.
- **2026-11-08: Always tear down RTSP audio sessions.** The owner's experience with ProScan is that a network glitch can leave the SDS200's audio dead until it's power-cycled. Every code path that opens an RTSP session, including tests, scripts, and failed runs, must send `TEARDOWN`. If audio wedges anyway, the fallback is a power cycle, so tell the owner rather than retrying in a loop. See design §8.1.
- **2026-11-08: Probe the real scanner read-only.** Safe commands are `MDL`, `VER`, `VOL`/`SQL` with no argument, `GSI`, `STS`, and RTSP `DESCRIBE` (no `SETUP`, so no session is left open). Anything that changes state needs the owner's OK first. Never guess at undocumented commands, such as the restart command, on the owner's scanner; capture ProScan's traffic instead.
- **2026-11-08: Community docs can be stale.** A forum post said the SDS200 doesn't stream RTSP audio over Ethernet. On firmware 1.24.00 it does. Trust hardware probes over forum posts, and record the firmware version with every finding.
- **2026-11-08: `STS` is the display, `GSI` is the data.** `STS` returns the screen text with the user's chosen field items and soft-key labels, but no colors. `GSI`/`PSI` return structured XML. `Index="4294967295"` means not programmed. See `docs/specs/2026-11-08-sds-remote-protocol.md`.
- **2026-11-08: The `sds200` Python library is the best protocol reference.** It's MIT-licensed, hardware-validated on the SDS200, and has extensive docs. Read it for facts, write our own Swift, and credit it.

- **2026-11-08: SDS200 1.24.00 `watch` observations.** `PSI,500` pushes about two updates per second here. While scanning with no signal, `RSSI` is `-999`, TGID can be the string ` ---` with a leading space, and `Name` attributes can be empty strings; the CLI trims text and shows `-` for blanks. `GSI` and `STS` replies each arrived as a single datagram, so the fragment-reassembly path still hasn't been exercised on hardware.
- **2026-11-10: The mock's reply delays run in real time, and a delayed UDP reply must tolerate the mock stopping (Plan 6 Task 2).** `MockClock` governs scenario content only, so `Options.replyDelays` (RTSP, and now UDP control by command name) is wall time even on a stepped clock; a test that wants "effect now, reply later" polls the mock's state (`menuPath`, `menuActions`) while the client's call is still pending. The delayed send runs on the control queue and does nothing once `stop()` has set the socket to -1, so stopping the mock mid-delay is clean (`stoppingDuringAReplyDelayIsClean`). Also: Swift Testing's `#expect(try await client.modelInfo().reported == ...)` fails to compile ("errors thrown from here are not handled") when the call returns a labelled tuple; bind it with `let` first. A drop fault ("the next datagram is lost") is armed only by `start()` and `setFaults(_:)`, not by `powerCycle()` or a reboot, so one that has fired isn't re-armed.

- **2026-11-10: A safety check made against the mirrored display can act on a screen that has already moved (Plan 6 Task 4 QA).** `MenuModel.press(.enterYes)` asked "is the highlighted row destructive?" of `session.display`, which is re-read only 150 ms after a key reply. A scroll followed at once by Enter checked the old row and ran Set All Lists Off with no confirmation (QA reproduced it 3 of 3 on the mock). Now `.enterYes` reads a fresh `STS` inside the action and checks that; if the read goes unanswered, nothing is sent. In the same round, `setValue` validated against the caller's copy of the input rules, so a stale or forged `MenuScreen` could send a value longer than the scanner's `MaxLength`; it now validates again against the rules of the screen the `MSI` re-check read.
  - What to do: a check that guards a state-changing command reads the scanner's state inside the same action, right before sending; never a cached view model or the caller's copy.

- **2026-11-10: A push can be older than a read's reply, so menu state comes only from reads (Plan 6 Task 4).** `PSI` pushes reach `StationSession` through the runner's stream (several actor hops), while a `GSI`/`MSI` reply goes straight back to the caller, so a push sent before a `KEY,M,P` can land on the main actor after the action's re-read. Setting `MenuModel.content` from pushes would flicker the menu out and back. Pushes only bring a read forward when their menu summary differs from the last read's, and one stale push costs one read, not one per tick (`hintServed`). Also, two of the re-check's guards are belt and braces that no test can isolate: `choose` requires `kind == .select`, but an entry screen has no `MenuItem`s, so the item check already fails; and the "still attached" check after a command is repeated by the re-read's own client fetch. Mutation runs on those two survive by design. The `isAlive` re-checks in `client(_:)` are not redundant (fix round 2): on a `KEY` action (`back`, `exit`, `press`, `enter`'s key, Enter after its `STS`) no read follows the client fetch, so the re-check after `await link.control()` is the only thing that stops a send after `detach()` (`detachDuringTheControlHopSendsNoKey`); before an `MSV`, the re-check read's own post-read check also covers it (`detachDuringTheRecheckSendsNothing`). - **2026-11-17: `ThrowingTaskGroup.waitForAll()` doesn't rethrow until every child has finished (Plan 18 Task 10).** `AudioPlayer.play` runs its chunk consumption and its configuration-change follower as two children of one group. - **2026-11-17: two ways a line-in source test passed for the wrong reason (Plan 18 Task 6).** (1) `configurationChangeRestartsOnce` first sent its three configuration changes to the first fake engine. - **2026-11-17: a mock tone's peak test needs a tolerance wider than the noise's worst sample (Plan 18 Task 4).** `MockLineOut` puts -52 dBFS Gaussian hiss (0.0025 RMS) under its tone. The first tests compared the captured peak with the tone's peak to within 0.01; they passed, then failed one run in three, because the sample nearest a sine's top lands on a noise sample of +4 sigma (about +0.01) over hundreds of periods. The noise was also what made volume 8 and above reach exactly +-1: the first version's `peak(forVolume:)` was `min(1, volume / 8)`, so the tone alone only touched full scale and the hiss pushed some samples past it to be clipped. Correction (final review, ruling R21): volume 8 already clips as far as the detector is concerned, since a sine peaking at 1.0 spends about five samples above 0.98855 at each crest at 440 Hz, so the mock's copy says "VOL 8 and up clips".
  - What to do: compare a noisy signal's peak with a tolerance of at least 5 sigma (0.02 here), and run a new timing-and-noise test five times before committing it. A test that wants clipping must have the noise on top, or a tone above 1.

- **2026-11-17: Line-in from the SDS150's headphone jack: what the owner's rig showed before Plan 18 was built (fw 1.01.02, a C-Media "USB Audio Device").** Throwaway probes (Core Audio listing, AVAudioEngine capture, and one using `NoctuleKit`'s `HitDetector` on `PSI,100` pushes beside the capture, on one clock; statistics only, no audio kept) found five things the design had guessed differently. (1) The interface is a **mono** mic input (1 channel, 48 kHz), so its "Left/Right" can't carry two scanners; that needs a stereo line-in. (2) Its Core Audio UID embeds the USB location (`AppleUSBAudioEngine:<maker>:<name>:<location>:2,1`), so it probably changes with the socket, like the SDS100's port path. (3) An `installTap` asked for 1024 frames delivered **4800-frame (100 ms)** buffers; an `AVAudioSinkNode` got the device's own **512-frame blocks every 10.67 ms**, perfectly regular. (5) Against the pushes, the audio of each of 6 hits (150 s, busy channels) lay **inside** the hit: it began 0.1–3.6 s after the first active push (median 2.1 s) and ended 3.5–5.2 s before the hit's end; two real hits (1.5 s and 2.0 s) carried no audio at all.   - What to do: capture with a sink node, not a tap, when latency or meter rate matters. Keep the recorder's `audioOffset` at 0 for the SDS150 line-in (the pre-roll is never needed to reach the audio; recordings carry a few seconds of dead air at each end). - **2026-11-09: "The latest close decides" let a clean close hide an unclean one (Plan 5 Task 1 QA I1/I2).** With a shorter wait after clean closes, keeping only the latest close per key meant a clean close 1 s after an unclean one (two stations sharing keys), or a clean close stamped in the future after the clock stepped back, erased the unclean close's 120 s: `claim` said `.claimed`. Now each record keeps the latest close *and* the latest unclean close, the wait is the longer of "full since the last unclean close" and "the latest close's own wait", and a future-stamped close counts as unclean now.
  - What to do: whenever a safety wait gets a "relaxed" case, keep the strict case's evidence separately so the relaxed one can never overwrite it, and probe with skewed clocks and interleaved writers.

- **2026-11-09: A cross-process cooldown needs check-and-claim under one lock; separate lock sections race.** `RTSPClient` used to read `lastClosed` (one `flock`), open the TCP connection, then write its lease (another `flock`). Two `noctule listen` runs waking from the same cooldown wait both passed the check before either wrote a lease, and both connected: `twoListensNeverConnectAtOnce` caught it on every run before the fix. Also, `recordClosed` cleared whatever lease was there, so one process's close erased another's crash lease. Now `RTSPCooldownStore.claim(hosts:pid:cooldown:)` checks and leases every cooldown key in one lock section, `recordClosed` clears only this process's lease, and a lock that can't be taken makes `claim` return `.unavailable` (no connect) instead of running unlocked (final review I1).
  - What to do: for any cross-process mutual exclusion, decide and record in the same critical section, and test it with real processes (or stores claiming for different pids), not two stores in one process, which share a pid and so share the lease.

- **2026-11-08: The cooldown file now carries a crash lease, so a crash or SIGKILL can't skip the 120 s wait (Plan 3 Task 5).** Before, only a recorded close started the cooldown, and a killed process records none. `RTSPClient` now writes `{"lease": {"openedAt", "pid"}}` right after the TCP connect, and every close clears it. A lease from another live process reads as "open right now" (full cooldown from now); a lease whose pid is gone (`kill(pid, 0)` gives `ESRCH`) is rewritten as "closed when discovered". `FileRTSPCooldownStore` holds `flock` on `<file>.lock` around every read-modify-write, including `lastClosed` (the dead-lease path writes), and surfaces write failures in `lastWriteError`. The old flat `host: ISO-8601` file is still read.
  - What to do: don't delete the `.lock` file or the lease by hand. Never call `kill(0, 0)` to probe a pid: 0 means the process group, so it would report a lease as forever alive (the store treats pid ≤ 0 as dead). A live pid alone doesn't prove the lease's owner is alive: pids get reused. The lease records the owner's start time (`sysctl` `KERN_PROC_PID`, `p_starttime`), and a live pid counts only if its start time matches within 1 s. Where the start time can't be read (iOS, `EPERM`) or wasn't recorded (older code), a lease counts only while it is younger than 12 h (`leaseMaxAge`). Without these checks, a reused pid would hold the cooldown forever and `noctule listen` would wait in a loop.
  - Test trap: fixture dates such as `Date(timeIntervalSince1970: 1_800_000_000)` are in 2027, in the future. A check like "a live lease returns at least now" passes or fails differently with them; use past dates for lease tests.

- **2026-11-08: The scanner sends G.711 µ-law at 8 kHz.** Record it as 16-bit PCM WAV, a lossless expansion. Don't transcode to AAC or MP3: lossy-on-lossy degrades weak transmissions.

- **2026-11-09: A reconnect is a stop then a start, so a user stop during its stop must win (Plan 4 final review C1).** `reconnect()` ran `halt()` then `start()`. A `stop()` arriving while that halt was tearing down (slow TEARDOWN) joined the same single-flight stop, and the "a later stop wins" counter was bumped before `start()` read it, so the reconnect started again right after. Through the registry that meant a window close, an edit or quit during a Reconnect left a windowless session streaming that wasn't in `entries` or `pendingStops`: `stopAll` returned clean and the app quit with RTSP open and no TEARDOWN (the wedge hazard), and after an edit the zombie raced the fresh session for the station lock. The same shape existed in `resume()`, which waits for a restart in flight and then started even if a `stop()` or a second sleep had come in. Now `SessionRegistry` marks every session it lets go of retired (terminal: `start`, `reconnect`, `resume` and `restartScanner` do nothing afterwards), and `reconnect()`/`resume()` capture stop and suspend generations before they wait and skip the start if either moved.
  - What to do: whenever a method awaits and then starts something, ask what a stop (or sleep) that arrives during the await should do, and capture a generation before the await rather than reading a counter that the awaited work itself bumps. An owner that drops an object for good should make that terminal on the object, not rely on bookkeeping that forgets it. Tests: `stopDuringReconnectHaltWins`, `reconnectThenReleaseStaysStopped`, `reconnectThenStopAllStaysStopped`, `startWhileRetiringOpensNothing`, `stopWhileResumeWaitsOnRestartStaysStopped`, `suspendWhileResumeWaitsOnRestartStaysSuspended`.

- **2026-11-09: Locks keyed by station don't stop two stations sharing a scanner (Plan 4 final review I1).** `StationLock` and the registry were keyed by station UUID, and the cooldown store treats a lease held by its own process as no obstacle, so two stations with the same host (easy: Settings › Import "keep existing" adds "<name> (imported)") opened two concurrent PSI subscriptions and RTSP sessions on one SDS200, which is untested (protocol §8 item 7) and treated as unsafe.   - What to do: guard the scanner, not the record that points at it. Two processes on two same-address stations are still not prevented (the lock is per station); a per-address cross-process lock would close that if it ever matters.

- **2026-11-09: A test-only override must also refuse the real data's location (Plan 4 final review M1).** `NOCTULE_COOLDOWN_SECONDS` and a private `NOCTULE_COOLDOWN_STORE` were honored whenever `NOCTULE_SETTINGS_DIR` was set, even to the standard `~/Library/Application Support/NoctuleScan`, which pairs the owner's real stations with a shortened cooldown. - **2026-11-18: Retrying a timed-out read at once only passes its late reply on (Plan 20 final review C1).** `ScannerClient` matches replies by command name, so a reply that comes after its read gave up answers the next request of that name. - **2026-11-19: Content that names its parent makes an in-place retry safe again (Plan 20, the SDS200's residual of C1).** The entry above stopped retrying reads in place, but a reply later than the timeout and the rest could still land on a same-kind `GLT` read for another parent, and on the SDS200's UDP every lost datagram then cost a list's whole read after a rest. The owner's trees showed every `Index` unique across the whole tree, so a `GLT` reply naming a system or department another parent is known to hold can be refused; tree switches wait until every list has been read once, so a stray whose owner wasn't read yet is caught when it is; and with that, `GLT` reads are retried once in place again. The first cut of the guard still let a stray through, and QA probes found three gaps: it ignored claims within the reading list (a late reply for a later system of the same list shifted the list's departments by one, and a click switched the wrong one), two lists whose cached claims crossed re-checked each other for ever, and a list that "kept failing" released the switches while a reply to its timed-out read was still out. Probe with a transport that holds, swallows or misdirects replies, not with mock delays. The packaging, cut-release, CI-workflow, signing, branding and icon tests waited for their tools by blocking (`run` → `waitFor` → `Thread.sleep`; elsewhere `waitUntilExit()`, `readDataToEndOfFile()`), and the serialized disk-image tests held one thread for the whole run, so the backlog of blocking tests drained through the other two while nothing else in the process ran: not the actor fakes, not any other test's `await`, not even the time limits (they fired at 119 s).   - What to do: a test never waits by blocking its thread: no `Thread.sleep`, `usleep`, `waitUntilExit()`, `readDataToEndOfFile()`, semaphore or group wait, in a synchronous test or an async one. `run`, `runScript`, `waitFor` and `Subprocess.finish()` are async and suspend (`finish()` waits for both pipes' end of file, not `readDataToEndOfFile()`), so a test that runs a tool is `async`; a one-time lookup is a `Task` its callers await, not a lazy global that blocks them. A blocking loop goes on a thread of its own (a fake's loop, a dispatch queue), marked `// off the cooperative pool`. `CooperativePoolTests` checks both: more tool runs than cores while a 50 ms sleep must end on time, and a scan of the target's sources. The suites that run many tools are `.serialized`, so they don't all start their tools at once on three cores.
