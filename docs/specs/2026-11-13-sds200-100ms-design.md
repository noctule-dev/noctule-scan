# The SDS200 at the SDS150's rates: `PSI,100` and `STS` every 100 ms

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: approved by the owner in chat, 2026-11-13; built on `feature/sds200-100ms`; hardware checks H1–H3 pending.

Labels: **(the owner's call)** is a decision the owner made; **(designer's call)** is a default the
designer chose that the owner hasn't ruled on. Everything not labeled is fact or a pointer.

## 1. Why

On 2026-11-13 the SDS150 moved to 100 ms status pushes (`PSI,100`) and to screen reads (`STS`) at
most once per 100 ms while the screen changes, after the owner saw the app trail the radio on a
fast scan at `PSI,500` (SDS150 design `2026-11-11-sds150-usb-design.md` §13 AC; LESSONS.md
2026-11-13). The SDS200 was left at 500 ms pushes and at most one `STS` a second, because neither
faster rate had been tried on it. The owner wants the SDS200 aligned with the SDS150, so both
radios follow a fast scan the same way in the app and in `noctule log`.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goal.** On an SDS200, the app's sessions and `noctule log` subscribe with `PSI,100`, and the
app's display loop reads `STS` at most once per 100 ms while pushes show the screen changing:
the values the SDS150 already uses. **(the owner's call, 2026-11-13)**

**Non-goals.**

- `noctule watch` keeps its own `--interval`, default 500, for every model, as it did when the
  SDS150 moved. Pass `--interval 100` to watch at the new rate. **(designer's call)**
- The SDS100 and the BCD436HP stay at 500 ms pushes and 1 s screen reads: neither has been tried
  faster. **(designer's call)**
- A name that is no known model keeps `StationRunner`'s 500 ms pushes. Its screen reads follow the
  app session's model, which falls back to the station's model override and then to the SDS200
  (`StationSession.model`), so they become 100 ms; the same goes for a station whose override is
  SDS200 while `MDL` names something unknown. This is accepted, not fixed: reads beyond the 5 s
  floor are brought forward only by a push that shows the screen changing, so at 500 ms pushes
  they stay at about two a second at most. **(designer's call, after the builder check)**
- The rates are per model, not per connection, so an SDS200 on USB serial (its profile lists
  `.usbSerial`) gets 100 ms too. That is untested and stays so: the owner's SDS200 runs over the
  network, and no SDS200 USB check is planned. **(designer's call, after the builder check)**
- The rest of `DisplayRefreshPolicy` is unchanged: the 5 s read of an unchanging screen, the
  150 ms after a key reply, and every menu timing (`menuMinInterval`, `menuIdleInterval`,
  `menuMaxKeyDeferral`). **(designer's call)**
- No new setting. The rate stays per-model data in `ModelProfile`, not a user preference.
  **(designer's call)**
- No change to audio, RTSP, the reconnect cooldown, hit detection, or the stall thresholds
  (`watch`'s max(5 s, 3 × interval) and the runner's are already sized for 100 ms by the SDS150).

**Anti-goals** (failure even if the code is right):

- Anything that makes the SDS200's control or audio less reliable. If a long hardware run (§6, H2)
  shows a hang, a dead control channel, or audio dropping where it didn't at 500 ms, the change is
  reverted, not tuned in place: the owner has to power-cycle the radio after a hang.
- Loosening tests to make them pass. A test that pinned the SDS200's 500 ms or 1 s pins 100 ms
  now; a test that counted reads or pushes over time at the old rate is updated to the new rate's
  numbers, not widened to accept both.

## 3. Hardware evidence so far

**The owner's ad-hoc check, 2026-11-13** (the throwaway branch `spike/sds200-100ms`, commit
`b064dfa`, the same one-line change, tests not run): the app on the owner's SDS200 over the
network, at `PSI,100` and `STS` up to every 100 ms. Audio played; the Scanner view kept up with the
radio's own screen. The owner then quit the app and launched it again, and control and audio both
came back. The run's length and the firmware weren't recorded (the last recorded firmware is
1.26.01). This is the first time `STS` has run beside `PSI` and an audio session on the SDS200 at
any rate: protocol notes `2026-11-08-sds-remote-protocol.md` §8 item 9 records it as never run.

**What the earlier hang does and doesn't say.** On 2026-11-08 the SDS200's whole network service
hung (UDP control dead, ping fine, power cycle needed) during 60 s audio sessions with `GSI` polled
once a second. The isolation that day traced it to RTSP on Network.framework: after `RTSPClient`
and `RTPReceiver` moved to BSD sockets, the same 60 s session with `GSI` every second passed
(protocol notes §6). So extra control traffic beside audio is not a known cause of a hang. But
nothing has run on the SDS200 at ten `STS` reads a second for longer than a few minutes, which is
what H2 checks.

**Not yet known:** the push rate the SDS200 actually gives at `PSI,100` (the SDS150 gave a push
every 116 ms, median; the SDS200 at `PSI,500` gave about two a second, LESSONS.md 2026-11-08),
and how it holds over an hour with audio, `STS` and hit logging.

**Also not yet known: control latency.** The client has one request slot, and a lost `STS` reply
holds it for the 2 s timeout. At about one lost UDP command in 12 and an `STS` every 100 ms while a
scan moves, a dead `STS` holds the slot roughly 60 % of the time (about 14 % at the old 1 s), so
keys, `VOL`/`SQL`, Hold and the menu's `GSI` may wait behind it, and keys beyond `maxQueuedKeys`
(8) are dropped. The ad-hoc check didn't look for this; H2's control step does.

## 4. The change

In `Sources/NoctuleKit/Models/ScannerModel.swift`, `ModelProfile.profile(for: .sds200)` gets
`pushInterval: .milliseconds(100)` and `displayRefreshInterval: .milliseconds(100)`. Nothing else in
the code path changes: `StationRunner` already subscribes at the profile's `pushInterval` for the
model `MDL` named, and `StationSession` already builds its display policy with
`DisplayRefreshPolicy(model:)`.

Comments that state the SDS200's rate or call 500 ms / 1 s "every other model" are corrected:
`ModelProfile.pushInterval` and `displayRefreshInterval` (`ScannerModel.swift`), the type comment of
`StationSession` (`StationSession.swift`, "1 s; 100 ms on the SDS150"), and `StationRunner`'s two
comments on the push interval (the type comment and the push-interval helper). "500 ms for a name
that is no known model" stays true.

## 5. Tests

These pin the SDS200's old rates and change to the new ones:

- `ModelProfileTests.pushIntervals` and `displayRefreshIntervals`: the SDS200 joins the SDS150 at
  100 ms; the SDS100 and BCD436HP stay at 500 ms and 1 s.
- `DisplayRefreshPolicyTests.modelsPolicy`: `DisplayRefreshPolicy(model: .sds200).minInterval` is
  100 ms; add the SDS100 (or BCD436HP) at 1 s so a model at the old rate is still covered.
- `StationRunnerTests.networkStationSubscribesAtTheSDS200sPushInterval`: expects `PSI,100`; its
  doc comment ("unchanged by the SDS150's faster rate") is rewritten.

These stay as they are: the `watch` tests that expect `PSI,500` (`WatchIntegrationTests`,
`MockScannerIntegrationTests`'s `control: PSI,500`), since `watch` keeps its default, and the
USB tests that set an explicit push interval.

The builder runs the whole suite (`swift test`) and the iOS build of `NoctuleAppCore`, and treats any
other failure as something the faster rate exposed: understand it before changing the test (see
the anti-goal in §2). The app tests that drive `MockSDS200` with a real display loop are the
likeliest place for one.

## 6. Hardware checks (the owner's session)

Run by the owner, or by an agent only when the owner asks, after the branch is built. All are
read-only apart from what the app and `log` normally send (`PSI`, `STS`, RTSP with TEARDOWN,
`PSI,0`). No `MSM,1`, `KEY` or `MSV`. Results go in protocol notes §8 item 9 and in §3 of this spec.

- **H1 — the push rate.** `noctule watch --interval 100 --count 300` with each line time-stamped
  on arrival (how is the builder's choice; a small script in the job's tmp directory is fine).
  Record the median and range of the gaps and `VER`. Passes if it exits 0 after 300 lines and
  `MDL` answers afterwards.
- **H2 — an hour in the app with audio.** The app on the SDS200 for an hour, audio playing,
  logging on, the Scanner view showing (so `STS` runs at its fastest when the scan moves). Passes
  if audio and the display keep going the whole hour, hits are logged, Quit exits cleanly, and
  `noctule info` answers afterwards. Any hang: stop, tell the owner, and the change is reverted
  (§2). Also while the scan is moving, press Hold, turn the volume knob, and enter and exit a menu
  a few times; note any delay over about 1 s or any key that doesn't take effect (a pass needs
  none). If controls lag, the owner chooses between reverting (§2) and a separate change (for
  example a shorter timeout for display reads); it isn't tuned on this branch.
- **H3 — quit and relaunch.** Already passed in the ad-hoc check (§3); repeat once after H2 on the
  built branch.

**The branch merges only after H2. (the owner's call, 2026-11-13)** The PR stays open until the
owner's hardware session; H1–H3's results go into the docs (§7) in the same PR. If H2 hangs, the
branch is dropped rather than merged.
